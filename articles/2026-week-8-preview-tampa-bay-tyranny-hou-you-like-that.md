# Tampa Bay Tyranny vs. HOU You Like That: The Week 8 Scouting Report

**Entering Week 8: Tampa Bay Tyranny (1–6) vs. HOU You Like That (2–5).**

One team arrives in search of its second victory; the other arrives with the confidence of the highest single-week score in the league. Tampa is 1–6, HOU 2–5, yet the difference in recent momentum feels larger than one win.

## The Matchup in Numbers

Through seven weeks, **Tampa Bay Tyranny** has scored **1669.26 total points**, an average of **238.47 per week**. **HOU You Like That** has scored **1701.56**, or **243.08 per week**. Those measures describe actual production, not a guarantee about this matchup. The most recent scoring split is revealing: Tampa Bay Tyranny scored **107.10 on offense** and **85.50 on defense** in Week 7, compared with HOU You Like That's **169.26 offensive** and **127.55 defensive** points.

HOU just produced 169.26 offensive and 127.55 defensive points. Tampa managed 107.10 and 85.50. Those one-week figures are not a forecast, but they establish a daunting benchmark for the Tyranny.

## What Tampa Bay Tyranny Must Do

Quinshon Judkins and Javonte Williams performed like the roster’s offensive leaders, and Jordyn Brooks contributed on defense. Those bright spots show the team is not without top-end talent. Joe Burrow and Dak Prescott give HOU two clear fantasy engines. Tampa needs another strong showing from Quinshon Judkins and Javonte Williams, accompanied by a more decisive contribution from its passing game and DL group.

The warning from Week 7 is just as instructive. The passing attack and defensive front failed to build around them: Garrett Wilson scored 3.60, Nik Bonitto 3.50 and Trey Hendrickson 4.00. A deep starting lineup magnifies every such shortfall. That is not a prediction that the same players will disappoint again; it is a reminder of where the starting lineup carried volatility at the last checkpoint.

## What HOU You Like That Must Do

HOU put its premium quarterback investment to work: Joe Burrow and Dak Prescott combined for 52.16 points, while Tee Higgins and Amon-Ra St. Brown sustained the receiving attack. Boye Mafe and Kamari Lassiter helped ensure the defense kept pace. Its manager also has to address the previous week’s problems: Jalen Coker’s 1.40 was an obvious soft spot, but the strength of the remaining starters left Seattle with no realistic path back. The result showed a very different ceiling from the team’s 2–5 record.

The available depth is a potential strength, but deep benches can make decisions harder rather than easier. The final starters will matter more than any hypothetical best lineup assembled after games end.
