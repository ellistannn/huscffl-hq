# Week 7: The Contenders Separate, but the Benches Have Their Say

Seven weeks into the season, the High Up Social Club Fantasy Football League has an unbeaten leader, an ambitious challenger and a middle class with reasons to believe. Week 7 supplied both the biggest rout and a game close enough to turn on one linebacker selection. **New York Noodles are 7–0; NJz Bunniez sit one game back at 6–1.** Their impending Week 8 clash is the kind of collision that gives an ordinary regular-season slate a postseason feel.


**On the actual NFL scoreboard:** Cincinnati’s 35–13 defeat of Baltimore and Detroit’s 20–17 victory over Green Bay were among Week 7’s notable results. Those games supplied fantasy storylines on different sides of the ball: HOU’s Joe Burrow scored 28.88, while New York’s Aidan Hutchinson scored 24.25. Real-life wins do not automatically translate into top fantasy scoring, but those players made the crossover unusually visible this week. ## The Week’s Defining Performances

**HOU’s 296.81 was the highest score on the slate.** Joe Burrow (28.88) and Dak Prescott (23.28) gave HOU two productive quarterback starts; Tee Higgins (23.80) and Amon-Ra St. Brown (19.70) provided receiving support. Boye Mafe (19.85) and Kamari Lassiter (19.25) kept the scoring balanced. The win was spectacular, but it arrived against a Seattle roster that had major shortcomings at tight end.

**New York did not need a perfect lineup to win by 102.39.** Drake Maye (27.44), Trey McBride (27.10), Aidan Hutchinson (24.25) and Tykee Smith (21.00) carried the visible highlights. Yet Yaya Diaby's 33.60, Jessie Bates III's 26.25 and Jamien Sherwood's 23.00 all sat on the Noodles' bench. It is hard to argue with a seven-game winning streak, but the details offer future opponents some hope.

**Pollard saved the Bunniez from an uncomfortable conversation.** Tony Pollard’s 35.00 and Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s 26.80 were vital in a win by 11.82. Blake Cashman scored an astonishing 53.40 on the NJz bench. The undefeated-versus-one-loss Week 8 showdown will test whether a manager can afford a repeat of that roster inefficiency.

**Defenders wrote the other headlines.** Alex Singleton’s 34.65 helped OKC survive LALALALA; Fred Warner answered with 25.00 for the losing side. Patrick Queen (26.55) supplied Utah’s strongest outing in a lopsided defeat. Quinnen Williams (21.80) and DeMarvion Overshown (21.15) drove Daebak's win over Tampa. In HUSCFFL, IDP is not a footnote—it is often the story.

## The Decision That Could Have Flipped a Game

Dallas scored 223.15 and lost by 11.82. Starting linebacker Gabe Jacas supplied only 3.50; reserve Christian Elliss scored 22.75. That legal single-position change would have improved Dallas by **19.25 points**, enough to reverse the result, all else unchanged. There is no way to know every outcome in advance, but there is a clear lesson: defensive depth must be managed as actively as offensive depth.

## What Each Result Changes

The Noodles maintain the league’s only undefeated record. The Bunniez remain the immediate pursuers. OKC, at 5–2, retained third place; Seattle, 4–3, has a chance to respond after a lopsided loss. At the other end, Tampa is 1–6, while HOU, Dallas and Utah are 2–5. Records can hide strong weekly scoring or brutal schedules, but eventually those teams need victories rather than encouraging totals.
