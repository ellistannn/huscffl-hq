# New York Noodles vs. NJz Bunniez: The Week 8 Scouting Report

**Entering Week 8: New York Noodles (7–0) vs. NJz Bunniez (6–1).**

The showdown that has been building since the opening weeks is finally here. The unbeaten Noodles have scored 2,124.06 through seven games, while the Bunniez have turned a 6–1 start into the only immediate threat to New York’s control of first place.

## The Matchup in Numbers

Through seven weeks, **New York Noodles** has scored **2124.06 total points**, an average of **303.44 per week**. **NJz Bunniez** has scored **1901.90**, or **271.70 per week**. Those measures describe actual production, not a guarantee about this matchup. The most recent scoring split is revealing: New York Noodles scored **152.92 on offense** and **124.75 on defense** in Week 7, compared with NJz Bunniez's **145.42 offensive** and **89.55 defensive** points.

## What New York Noodles Must Do

The Noodles are winning with depth across both sides of the ball, not a single runaway scorer. Their defensive starters alone put up more than 120 points in Week 7, a reminder that one of the league’s best offenses also carries a meaningful IDP advantage. New York will want Trey McBride, Drake Maye and its defensive anchors firing simultaneously. NJz needs Tony Pollard and Jaxon Smith-Njigba to sustain their form while avoiding the weak starter outputs that nearly cost it against Dallas.

The warning from Week 7 is just as instructive. The offense was not flawless: Derrick Henry, Tetairoa McMillan and Kenneth Walker III each delivered single-digit weeks, while Julian Love barely registered in the secondary. Against a stronger opponent, those gaps become more consequential. That is not a prediction that the same players will disappoint again; it is a reminder of where the starting lineup carried volatility at the last checkpoint.

## What NJz Bunniez Must Do

The Bunniez escaped their narrowest sort of danger thanks to Tony Pollard’s 35.00 and Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s 26.80. Their star skill players created the cushion that protected an uneven roster elsewhere. Its manager also has to address the previous week’s problems: Nico Collins and the starting defensive front failed to produce expected support, and the most glaring number of all belonged to unused linebacker Blake Cashman: 53.40 on the bench. That is not just a what-if; it is a warning about the difficulty of managing a deep IDP roster.

The available depth is a potential strength, but deep benches can make decisions harder rather than easier. The final starters will matter more than any hypothetical best lineup assembled after games end.
