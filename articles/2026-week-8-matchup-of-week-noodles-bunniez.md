# Matchup of the Week: Noodles vs. Bunniez Is the First True Test at the Top

**New York Noodles (7–0) vs. NJz Bunniez (6–1). Week 8, HUSCFFL Matchup of the Week.**

Some fantasy rivalries become meaningful because of a history of close finishes. Others get there because the standings leave no alternative. In the first seven weeks of 2026, the Noodles and Bunniez have made themselves the two teams everyone else is trying to catch. At the next checkpoint, one of them will have tested the other directly.

New York has outscored the entire league through Week 7: **2,124.06 total points**, an average of **303.44 per matchup**. NJz has compiled **1,901.90**, averaging **271.70**. That 31.74-point gap per game helps explain the undefeated record, but the Bunniez' six wins establish that this is not a ceremonial coronation.

## The Favorite: Noodles Have Answers on Both Sides

In its 277.67–175.28 rout of Utah, New York got **152.92 points from offense and 124.75 from defense**. Drake Maye (27.44) provided elite quarterback production, Geno Smith (23.48) delivered from superflex and Trey McBride (27.10) showed why an impact tight end is especially dangerous under TE premium. Aidan Hutchinson (24.25) and Tykee Smith (21.00) reinforced the other half of the roster.

There were vulnerabilities. Derrick Henry (5.40), Kenneth Walker III (7.70) and Tetairoa McMillan (6.00) did not match the higher-end performances elsewhere in the lineup. Julian Love (1.50) barely contributed. New York still won by more than 100, but such uneven starts could be much harder to absorb against a 6–1 opponent.

The largest warning was on the bench: Yaya Diaby (33.60), Jessie Bates III (26.25) and Jamien Sherwood (23.00) went unused. The Noodles’ calculated Week 7 optimal lineup exceeded their actual score by **89.45 points**. That is impressive depth and a caution about the importance of selection—both at once.

## The Challenger: Bunniez' Stars Can Erase a Deficit Quickly

The Bunniez did not dominate Dallas. They survived, 234.97–223.15, behind Tony Pollard’s **35.00** and Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s **26.80**. Jonathan Taylor (18.00) and Bijan Robinson (14.70) supplemented an offense that contributed **145.42** points. Bobby Okereke (19.75) and Payton Wilson (18.55) helped a defensive unit that scored **89.55**.

The concern is that New York’s Week 7 defense scored 35.20 points more than NJz's. The Bunniez cannot count on their offensive stars to erase that shortfall indefinitely. Better output from the starting line and secondary is essential.

Then there is Blake Cashman. His **53.40 points on the bench** were among the most striking numbers of the entire week. The Bunniez also had Marcus Jones (23.20) and Harrison Smith (20.50) outside their starting lineup. The calculated gap between their starting score and eligible optimal lineup was **127.90**. Not every hindsight alternative is a reasonable pregame decision, but the roster's usable defensive depth is precisely what must be converted into points against New York.

## Three Battles That Could Decide It

**1. The quarterback and tight-end floor.** New York got 78.02 combined from Maye, Smith and McBride in Week 7. NJz needs dependable output from C.J. Stroud (16.52), Kyler Murray (13.90) and Isaiah Likely (3.20), plus enough upside at the other skill positions to counterbalance the gap.

**2. The defensive lineup call.** Noodles' Hutchinson and Tykee Smith set a high weekly benchmark. Bunniez' Okereke and Wilson were productive, but the immense unused Cashman performance illustrates how high the stakes of a linebacker start can be. The most important managerial decision might happen before an NFL football is kicked.

**3. The damage from low-scoring starters.** New York survived several single-digit skill players; NJz survived Nico Collins' 1.60, Mason Graham's zero and Rueben Bain Jr.'s 0.75. Whichever team removes more of those holes from its weekly score has a path to control the contest.

## What a Win Would Mean

A **Noodles victory** would move New York to 8–0, push NJz to 6–2 and strengthen the case that the league's early leader is more than a product of its schedule. A **Bunniez victory** would make both clubs 7–1 and recast the top of the standings as a genuine head-to-head race. Neither hypothetical outcome is a prediction.

It is rare for a regular-season fantasy matchup to offer such a clean contrast: an unbeaten frontrunner with leading cumulative points against a challenger with explosive offensive stars and a huge reserve of defensive talent. **This is the week when the story of the season can change.**
