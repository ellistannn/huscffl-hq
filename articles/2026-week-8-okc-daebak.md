# OKC Keeps Pace as Daebak’s Offense Falters

A win in the middle of a crowded playoff race is worth more than style points, and **Good Things We OKC! collected its sixth victory with a 253.66–200.55 win over District of Daebak**. OKC moves to **6–2**; District slips to **3–5**, still in the chase but short on margin for error.

The difference was an extraordinarily productive defensive cast. Alex Singleton produced **28.60** from the IDP flex, Quentin Lake scored **25.25**, and Roquan Smith added **24.75**. That trio alone delivered **78.60**, giving OKC a platform even without a massive quarterback outing. Chuba Hubbard’s **21.40** anchored the offense.

District generated individual resistance. Quinnen Williams scored **27.55**, Dexter Lawrence II added **22.35** in the IDP flex, and Jordan Love supplied **21.52** in the superflex. But a few defensive stars could not pull the whole starting lineup over the line. Justin Jefferson managed **11.80**, while James Cook III contributed **17.10**.

The most painful number for District may be the opportunity left unused. Its calculated optimal lineup exceeded the actual score by **120.92 points**, the largest such gap in Week 8. Emmett Johnson scored **22.80**, K’Lavon Chaisson **21.35**, and Andrew Mukuba **20.00** on the bench. This is hindsight, not proof that any particular start was obvious, but the available depth underscores the stakes of getting the final lineup right.

OKC now gets the undefeated New York Noodles in Week 9. District faces LALALALA in a matchup with direct implications for the middle of the standings. If District is to turn its defensive talent into a genuine playoff push, it needs a more complete starting performance immediately.
