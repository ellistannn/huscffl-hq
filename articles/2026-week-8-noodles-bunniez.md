# The Streak Survives Its Biggest Test: Noodles Handle Bunniez

The league’s unbeaten leader faced one of its strongest challengers in Week 8. Instead of a late-game thriller, **New York Noodles delivered a 291.77–217.55 victory over NJz Bunniez**, extending the defending champion’s start to **8–0** and leaving NJz at **6–2**.

New York was strongest where championship teams often distinguish themselves: across both halves of its lineup. The offense contributed **161.52**, the defense **130.25**, and neither side needed a single superhuman score to carry the week. Bhayshul Tuten delivered **28.90** in a flex spot, Josh Allen supplied **26.56**, and Drake Maye added **22.46** in the superflex. Up front, Greg Rousseau (**25.15**) and Jeffery Simmons (**23.35**) turned the defensive line into a decisive advantage.

NJz was not without answers. Tony Pollard scored **27.20**, Jaxon Smith-Njigba added **20.80**, and quarterbacks Kyler Murray (**19.90**) and Trevor Lawrence (**19.40**) kept the offense moving. But the Bunniez never received the same sustained production from the remainder of the starting lineup. The 74.22-point margin was the widest in the league this week.

There is also a managerial footnote on both sides. New York had George Karlaftis (**28.50**) and Adonai Mitchell (**23.70**) on the bench, while NJz left Cameron Heyward’s **25.70** unused. These were not games lost or won by one substitution; the Noodles had the broader scoring edge.

For NJz, this loss does not erase an impressive first half. It does, however, sharpen the question of how to close the gap with the champion. New York’s reward is an even bigger target on its back: **Good Things We OKC!**, another 6–2 contender, is next.
