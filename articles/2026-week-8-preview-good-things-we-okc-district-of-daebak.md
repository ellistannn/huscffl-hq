# Good Things We OKC! vs. District of Daebak: The Week 8 Scouting Report

**Entering Week 8: Good Things We OKC! (5–2) vs. District of Daebak (3–4).**

OKC has settled into third at 5–2, while Daebak is trying to convert an encouraging 3–4 record into a climb toward the playoff picture. This is a test of whether OKC can keep pace with the top two and whether Daebak can consistently beat strong opposition.

## The Matchup in Numbers

Through seven weeks, **Good Things We OKC!** has scored **1801.36 total points**, an average of **257.34 per week**. **District of Daebak** has scored **1738.33**, or **248.33 per week**. Those measures describe actual production, not a guarantee about this matchup. The most recent scoring split is revealing: Good Things We OKC! scored **135.36 on offense** and **116.70 on defense** in Week 7, compared with District of Daebak's **126.96 offensive** and **98.70 defensive** points.

Week 7 highlighted the importance of defense for both sides. Alex Singleton’s 34.65 gave OKC a difference-maker; Quinnen Williams and DeMarvion Overshown delivered over 20 apiece for Daebak. The team that wins the middle of the IDP lineup may control the outcome.

## What Good Things We OKC! Must Do

Alex Singleton’s 34.65 was an exceptional IDP swing, and Caleb Williams, CeeDee Lamb and Christian McCaffrey gave OKC an offensive counterweight. The roster’s best players delivered in both scoring categories. OKC seeks steadier production from quiet spots around CeeDee Lamb and Christian McCaffrey. Daebak needs bigger contributions from Justin Jefferson and Courtland Sutton without losing the defensive floor that beat Tampa.

The warning from Week 7 is just as instructive. Tremaine Edmunds posted a zero and Malaki Starks finished with only 2.25. The bench included Demetrius Knight Jr., Carl Granderson and Anthony Hill Jr., all of whom could have strengthened the defensive tally in hindsight. That is not a prediction that the same players will disappoint again; it is a reminder of where the starting lineup carried volatility at the last checkpoint.

## What District of Daebak Must Do

Quinnen Williams and DeMarvion Overshown supplied a defensive foundation, while Cam Skattebo and Blake Corum helped the backfield and Matthew Stafford/Jordan Love provided usable quarterback output. Its manager also has to address the previous week’s problems: There were still disappointing marquee performances from Justin Jefferson and Courtland Sutton, plus quiet scores from Budda Baker and Will Anderson Jr. Daebak won despite those gaps, not because they disappeared.

The available depth is a potential strength, but deep benches can make decisions harder rather than easier. The final starters will matter more than any hypothetical best lineup assembled after games end.

## Key Players to Watch

- **Good Things We OKC!:** **Alex Singleton (34.65)**, **Caleb Williams (22.12)**, **CeeDee Lamb (21.90)**, **Quay Walker (19.50)**. Each supplied a foundation in Week 7 and should shape the manager’s expectations, subject to availability and NFL matchups.
- **District of Daebak:** **Quinnen Williams (21.80)**, **DeMarvion Overshown (21.15)**, **Cam Skattebo (18.60)**, **Matthew Stafford (17.50)**. The question is which performances repeat, and which teammates can close the gaps around them.
- **The IDP swing:** One linebacker or defensive back producing a 20-plus-point day can change a matchup. Last week’s scores underline how much defensive lineup quality matters in HUSCFFL.

## The Storyline to Follow

At 5–2, OKC has earned its place as the closest pursuer behind the top two. Daebak will test whether that advantage is durable. The next test is much tougher: third-place OKC has been collecting wins behind high-impact performances on both sides of the ball.
