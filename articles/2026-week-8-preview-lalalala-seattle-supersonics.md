# LALALALA vs. Seattle Supersonics: The Week 8 Scouting Report

**Entering Week 8: LALALALA (3–4) vs. Seattle Supersonics (4–3).**

Seattle returns at 4–3 after taking an 82.24-point hit from HOU. LALALALA is 3–4 despite scoring more cumulative points than some teams above it. This matchup offers each manager a route toward turning a mixed season into a stronger second half.

## The Matchup in Numbers

Through seven weeks, **LALALALA** has scored **1807.02 total points**, an average of **258.15 per week**. **Seattle Supersonics** has scored **1755.55**, or **250.79 per week**. Those measures describe actual production, not a guarantee about this matchup. Fred Warner was the defensive centerpiece, with Quincy Williams and Chase Young also producing. LALALALA’s defensive starters outscored its offensive starters, an unusual split that almost kept the team level with OKC. Fred Warner, Quincy Williams and Chase Young must maintain the IDP advantage for LALALALA. Seattle needs Luther Burden III and its quarterback starters to remain productive while improving at tight end after a combined 4.50 from its two starters in Week 7.

The warning from Week 7 is just as instructive. The offense was less dependable. Tyler Shough and Brock Purdy combined for only 25.84, and several flex and receiver starts failed to produce the explosive plays needed to offset OKC’s Singleton eruption. That is not a prediction that the same players will disappoint again; it is a reminder of where the starting lineup carried volatility at the last checkpoint.

## What Seattle Supersonics Must Do

Seattle had individual success stories, particularly Luther Burden III and Nakobe Dean, while Kirk Cousins and Jared Goff kept the superflex structure functional. Those were useful contributions, just not the kind that survive a 296-point opponent. Its manager also has to address the previous week’s problems: Two tight ends, Juwan Johnson and Sam LaPorta, combined for just 4.50 points. Malik Willis’ 26.02 and Keisean Nixon’s 20.00 were left unused, creating a lineup-efficiency subplot in an otherwise one-sided game.

The available depth is a potential strength, but deep benches can make decisions harder rather than easier. The final starters will matter more than any hypothetical best lineup assembled after games end.
